## Livtet, Who Art Thou?

Livtet is a project I've mulled [over][1] and [over][2] (and [over][3] - and
[over][4]!); starting it from scratch when I discovered a new form of
organizing content that worked with my sense of library sciences. I've decided
to take a _hard shift_ and put it into the public again. It used to live on
SourceHut but I brought it to GitHub so I can take advantage of a few things:

- Sponsorship: this project is not cheap to develop and I want it to survive
- Availability: it's one thing to work with advocates of AT on
  <https://tangled.org> but it's another to get the platform that holds everyone
  who considers themselves a member of the open source community on it.
- Collaboration: It's not to say that SourceHut and Tangled aren't
  collaborative platforms; we as a developer community were so burned by
  SourceForge that we let GitHub become [our one and only "VCS"][^1].

## Protocol Implementation Driven-Development (PID<sup>2</sup>)

With all that said, Livtet has become my gateway project into the _complexity_
of [AT Protocol][5]. I have plans to have it work with Nostr soon and had a
small prototype generated by DeepSeek but I need to scale up infrastructure to
handle both[^2]. In order to _really_ operate in a communal fashion, I decided, after some conversation of not taking the initial approach that applications like Bookhive and Popfeed have in
making custom lexicons for their tracking of information, and leaned into the
spirit of <https://lexicon.community> in a small prototype called
[Bibliograph][6]. I initially had a custom lexicon for it under the NSID
`net.olamaelcu.livtet` but interoperability is _way more_ important to me.

Bibliograph is the result of conversation with folks interested in tracking
books on-protocol and some early research on what that'd look like for a real
application, like [Livtet][7] to make use of it. For example, [my profile page][8]
shows information that apps like Bookhive and Popfeed can start consuming to
show today, thanks to the XRPC methods provided by Bibliograph. You can see the
conversation that sparked it on [AT Protocol Discourse][11] - be sure to chime in!

One could eschew using Bibliograph and ingest the AT firehose themselves to
compose a graph of information about books and the information around it today,
combing over existing lexicons that emit such information so it provides a
"community-powered backfill", so to speak. However, having folks move to the
new lexicon for _new work_ helps test out the kinks in the spec and schema.

## First Release?

This brings me to the more interesting page for layfolks: Livtet's web
experience should have an early release sometime this month. I'm slating it for
late August as I'm also trying to work on the native mobile applications to go
with it as well. Those are actually [open source][9] as I hope to demonstrate
how to use the core library with it but it'll support communicating to the
Livtet API (to be documented) as well as eventually using the Bibliograph XRPC
directly, making it a "native" AT Protocol client. I'd love to see clients like
[Heron][10] sneak in support for this, the same way it supports Standard Site
subscriptions as this will show the plural power of AT Protocol and the things
you can unlock with a simple API.
